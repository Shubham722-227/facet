## 1. Executive Summary

FACET Verify is planned as a due diligence, investigative intelligence
and risk assessment company. Its website should help organisations
understand what FACET investigates, what a client receives, how the
process works, and how to request a scoped engagement.

The site must not look like a real-estate portal, a generic
background-check form, or a flashy cyber/crypto product. It should feel
like a clean, discreet, evidence-led professional services firm
supported by modern technology.

### Recommended positioning

**Brand descriptor:** Due Diligence & Investigative Intelligence

**Primary brand line:** Make Confident Decisions. See the Full Picture.

**Supporting statement:** FACET Verify combines structured research,
public-record verification and investigative analysis to help clients
understand property, people, businesses, legal exposure and transaction
risk before making important decisions.

**Alternative hero headline:** Due Diligence Beyond the Documents.

**Core promise:** Clear scope. Sourced findings. Practical risk context.

Do not promise that every risk can be found, that every record is
complete, or that a report guarantees a safe transaction. Describe the
work as risk identification and decision support based on a defined
scope, accessible sources and available evidence.

### The seven confirmed service verticals

1.  Property & Real Estate Due Diligence
2.  Seller, Promoter & Landowner Due Diligence
3.  Litigation, Legal & Regulatory Due Diligence
4.  Vendor, Supplier & Third-Party Due Diligence
5.  Fraud Risk & Investigative Intelligence
6.  M&A, Investment & Transaction Due Diligence
7.  Asset, Ownership & Wealth Intelligence

These are the seven services selected for the initial FACET Verify
portfolio. The site should not advertise unrelated standalone service
lines unless the company later decides to add them and confirms the
necessary capabilities.

## 2. Research Summary and Design Lessons

The following websites were used as strategic references, not as
templates to copy:

-   RA Due Diligence --- https://www.raduediligence.com/
-   Vcheck Global --- https://vcheckglobal.com/
-   Alias Intelligence --- https://aliasintelligence.com/
-   Kroll --- https://www.kroll.com/en

### Vcheck Global --- https://vcheckglobal.com/

**Observed useful patterns** - Connects pre-transaction diligence with
ongoing monitoring. - Presents a mix of investigations, background
checks, identity verification and monitoring. - Explains how its
platform supports human investigation. - Uses use cases and
industry-specific messaging. - Offers a due-diligence scope-finding
experience instead of relying only on a static contact form. -
Highlights source trails, investigation status and configurable risk
thresholds.

**What FACET should adapt** - A guided "Build Your Due Diligence Scope"
flow. - A clear separation between services, industries and use cases. -
A sample report preview showing source references, findings, flags and
risk context. - A future path to monitoring and client-portal features,
without implying these already exist.

### Alias Intelligence --- https://aliasintelligence.com/

**Observed useful patterns** - Investigator-led positioning supported by
technology. - A clean path from services to industries and contact/demo
actions. - A client-portal visual that makes the deliverable tangible. -
A focus on source-linked findings, human review and flexible scope. -
Concise calls to action and content aimed at high-stakes decisions.

**What FACET should adapt** - Keep human judgment and evidence quality
central to the story. - Show a report or dashboard preview, not only
decorative 3D imagery. - Make each service page explain what is
investigated, why it matters and what the client receives. - Use
practical, specific language instead of generic claims such as
"next-generation solutions."

### Kroll --- https://www.kroll.com/en

**Observed useful patterns** - Organises a broad professional-services
portfolio into understandable service families. - Emphasises expert
judgement, actionable intelligence, investigations and risk
management. - Uses research, insights and case material to demonstrate
depth. - Maintains a formal, institutional tone suitable for financial,
legal and corporate clients.

**What FACET should adapt** - Present FACET as a serious investigative
and due-diligence firm, not just a software tool. - Build a knowledge
centre that supports service pages. - Keep the visual design restrained
and the language precise. - Distinguish evidence, analysis and
recommendations in report examples.

### RA Due Diligence --- https://www.raduediligence.com/

The site was included in the requested reference set. At the time of
preparing this document, the available search results did not provide
enough reliably accessible page content to make specific claims about
its individual design or service features. Review it manually during
final design discovery before treating any particular feature as a
confirmed reference.

### Research-derived principles

1.  Lead with the client's decision and risk question, not with a long
    list of checks.
2.  Show the difference between record retrieval and investigative
    analysis.
3.  Make the scope, source trail, report structure and limitations
    understandable.
4.  Organise services both by service type and by client need.
5.  Use case studies only when real and approved; label illustrative
    scenarios clearly.
6.  Do not use competitor statistics, client logos, testimonials or
    claims as FACET claims.
7.  A quote builder should request enough information to scope work, but
    should not display unvalidated prices or turnaround promises.

## 3. Brand and Messaging Framework

### Brand personality

-   Clear and composed
-   Investigative and evidence-led
-   Modern, but not flashy
-   Confidential and professional
-   Independent and careful
-   Practical and decision-oriented

### Messaging pillars

**1. Evidence before assumption**\
Findings should be linked to identified sources where possible, with
source limitations recorded.

**2. Context beyond records**\
Where the engagement calls for it, investigation can help interpret
relationships, discrepancies and patterns that a simple document search
may not explain.

**3. Risk made understandable**\
Reports should organise findings into a summary, relevant risk themes,
supporting detail and suggested next steps.

**4. Defined scope and transparency**\
Clients should understand what was searched, what was not searched,
which jurisdictions and dates were covered, and what limitations apply.

**5. Technology with accountable review**\
Technology can support research, workflow and reporting; it should not
be presented as a substitute for professional judgement.

### Approved-style language

Prefer: - "helps identify potential risks" - "based on the agreed
scope" - "publicly available and lawfully accessible sources" -
"source-linked findings, where available" - "investigative analysis" -
"risk indicators" - "findings for further review" - "estimated
turnaround, subject to scope and source availability"

Avoid: - "100% fraud-proof" - "guaranteed clean title" - "we find every
hidden asset" - "complete criminal history" unless the scope and source
coverage support it - "certified title" unless FACET is legally entitled
and professionally qualified to provide that certification - "real-time
monitoring" unless a working monitoring service exists - invented
volume, success-rate, turnaround or client statistics

### Primary calls to action

-   **Get a Quote** --- primary
-   **Discuss Your Requirement** --- secondary
-   **Explore Services** --- tertiary
-   **View Sample Report** --- only after an approved sample has been
    prepared
-   **Client Login** --- show only once a secure portal exists

## 4. Website Design Direction

### Design objective

Create a clean, editorial, premium B2B website. Use 3D selectively to
communicate property, people, connections and risk. The design should
prioritise legibility and confidence over visual effects.

### Recommended visual system

**Base palette** - Midnight navy: `#0B1422` - Deep slate: `#142235` -
White: `#FFFFFF` - Off-white page background: `#F5F7FA` - Body text:
`#263445` - Muted text: `#64748B` - Border: `#DCE3EB` - FACET red
accent: `#E52B32` - Verified/success: `#26865A` - Warning: `#C98316` -
High-risk indicator: `#D92D3A`

Use red sparingly for the primary CTA, alert states and small brand
details. Do not use red as a general decorative colour. Green should
mean verified or positive only when the underlying data supports it.

**Typography** - Use a modern sans-serif such as Inter, Manrope or a
comparable accessible typeface. - Use strong but restrained heading
weights. - Body copy should be comfortably readable at 16--18 px on
desktop. - Avoid tiny uppercase labels for essential information. - Keep
line lengths moderate, especially on service pages.

**Layout** - Desktop content width: approximately 1120--1240 px. - Use a
12-column grid for large screens. - Generous whitespace, consistent
vertical rhythm and clear section headings. - Prefer simple cards with
fine borders over heavy shadows. - Use alternating white/off-white and
dark-navy sections to establish hierarchy. - Mobile layout must be
designed intentionally, not merely compressed.

### 3D and visual assets

Use 3D in four places: 1. Hero: one architectural property model with a
small number of floating evidence cards. 2. Service cards: small,
consistent 3D illustrations. 3. Report preview: a 3D folder or layered
report object, used sparingly. 4. Process section: small icons or
objects connected by a simple line.

Use realistic architectural geometry, subtle material depth and soft
lighting. Avoid excessive neon, overlaid panels that obscure copy, or 3D
objects on every section.

**Accessibility and performance** - Provide static fallbacks for 3D
assets. - Respect reduced-motion preferences. - Lazy-load below-the-fold
images and 3D. - Compress images and avoid heavy real-time 3D unless
performance is tested. - Do not use colour alone to communicate risk. -
Ensure keyboard navigation, visible focus states, form labels and
appropriate contrast.

### Section 5: How it works

**Heading:** A Clear Process from Scope to Findings.

**Step 2 --- Agree the Scope**\
We confirm the subject identifiers, jurisdictions, research areas,
deliverables, exclusions, fees and expected timeline.

**Step 3 --- Research and Verify**\
The agreed research is carried out using relevant public records,
client-provided material and other lawfully accessible sources
appropriate to the engagement.

**Step 4 --- Analyse and Document**\
Relevant findings are cross-referenced, limitations are recorded, and
risk indicators are separated from confirmed facts and allegations.

**Step 5 --- Deliver the Report**\
Receive a structured report with an executive summary, findings, source
notes where available, scope limitations and recommended next steps for
further review.

## 14. FAQs Page

**URL:** `/faqs`

### What does FACET Verify do?

FACET Verify provides scoped due diligence and investigative research
across property, counterparties, litigation, third parties, transactions
and asset ownership.

### Does FACET Verify provide legal advice?

The website should describe the service as due diligence and research
support. Formal legal opinions and representation should be provided by
qualified legal professionals.

### Can you guarantee that a property has a clear title?

No report should be described as a guarantee unless the company is
legally authorised and professionally qualified to provide that specific
guarantee. Research findings are limited to the agreed scope, accessible
records and available evidence.

### How long does an investigation take?

The timeline depends on scope, jurisdiction, number of subjects, source
availability and urgency. FACET should provide an estimate after
reviewing the requirement.

### How much does due diligence cost?

Pricing depends on the scope, jurisdictions, number of subjects and
depth of research. Request a quote for a defined proposal.

### What information do you need to begin?

Typically, the subject's name or property identifier, relevant
location/jurisdiction, the purpose of the research and any supporting
documents the client is authorised to share.

### Are all records available online?

No. Availability, completeness, searchability and access rules vary by
source and jurisdiction.

### How do you handle confidential information?

Describe the actual data-handling practices, access controls, retention
policy and contractual terms once these have been established. Do not
claim certifications or security controls that have not been
independently verified.

### Can you investigate companies and people outside India?

Only offer cross-border work where the company has confirmed source
access, suitable partners and the ability to comply with relevant local
laws. Confirm scope on a case-by-case basis.

### Does a report prove fraud or misconduct?

Not automatically. Reports should distinguish verified records,
allegations, analytical indicators and matters requiring further
investigation.
